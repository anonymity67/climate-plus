      Field process from normal service to recovery

      Use one ward and one duty coordination point for the first pilot. A named incident lead owns a single operational record. Each module adds linked work orders with its own owner, status, evidence, and due time. Keep the existing local incident command, public messaging, and water release authority in charge.

      1. Prepare2. Verify disruption3. Assign repair4. Plan backup 5. Authorize dispatch6. Reconcile receipts7. Restore service8. Handover

      1. Prepare the ward

      The incident lead coordinates with mapping staff, school staff, and the water authority.

      Approve the ward boundary, contact roster, backup teams, available vehicles, source approval process, operating hours, accessible pickup criteria, incident categories, loss-of-connectivity procedure, and local supply assumptions. Register each asset, school tank, source and pickup point. Walk the most important backup links with field staff and an accessibility representative. Print a dated ward map and prepare numbered blank forms.

      Hand over the signed pilot charter, verified map, opening stock for each source and store, role assignments, and approved service and escalation policy.

      2. Capture, deduplicate, and verify the disruption

      The caretaker reports the problem, and the duty dispatcher verifies and logs it.

      Capture asset ID or nearest verified landmark, observation time, type and severity of outage, estimated households and critical facilities affected, reported alternatives, road reports, and a callback or staff role reference if needed. Accept paper, phone or offline forms. Search open cases by asset ID and event window. Attach a second observation to the active case unless the dispatcher confirms a distinct event. Corroborate reports via an authorized field observer without sending anyone into unsafe conditions. A shared incident ID ties the outage, closures, school stock checks, and supply actions together.

      Record the verified incident ID, independent observation or named verifier, affected zones, linked assets, and a review deadline for provisional or contested reports.

      3. Send the ServiceRelay repair order

      The repair coordinator assigns the order and the technician confirms receipt.

      Rank confirmed outages using affected households, severity, lack of alternate service, critical users, and time down. Use priority weights agreed with local operators. Escalate isolated groups or critical facilities using local procedures. Record crew acceptance, tools or parts needed, estimated arrival, and next review. Keep one active repair order per distinct asset incident. Record waiting states for blocked access, unsafe conditions, parts or crew. Feed the expected service restoration time back into the backup-supply horizon.

      Record the assigned crew, acknowledgement time, estimated arrival, any blockers, and the next contact.

      4. Build a joint household and school supply proposal

      The dispatcher prepares the proposal with the school coordinator, route verifier, and stock custodian.

      FloodRoute. Load approved pickup sites and road segments with time, travel mode, and accessibility status. Compute the number of eligible slots as the lower of released volume divided by the approved allotment and verified staffing/throughput within the window. Assign households to eligible sites, protect access for those requiring assistance, then use travel cost to break ties. Report unmet households by group and request assisted delivery, a staffed mobile point, or another authorized option.

      WASH Continuity. Ask schools to report usable water by approved purpose and tank, attendance and shelter occupancy, anticipated usage, tank headroom, operating schedule, and time until the next reliable supply. Estimate a shortfall per service and time window. Record drinking water availability, water and soap for handwashing, usable toilets, and accessibility separately. Escalate consumables or facility failures to the education/WASH lead.

      Shared ledger. Custodian confirms released stock by lot, destination use, and window. Apply the policy approved for this incident. The drill first protects an explicit contingency reserve and a capped school planning floor, then tries to cover household fallback and allocates remaining eligible volume to schools. In the field, the incident lead agrees protection for essential demand across both groups and records any tradeoffs or override. Never count the same school courtyard stock as institutional storage.

      Submit a versioned proposal with group assignments by time window, school orders by tank and use, inventory checks by lot, and owners for any unmet demand. Local authorities set actual water service levels and emergency allocation criteria. The tabletop uses illustrative volumes and allotments. Sphere Handbook · JMP school WASH definitions

      5. Authorize, reserve, and dispatch the exact plan

      Once the incident lead authorizes the plan, the custodian reserves stock and the dispatcher issues orders.

      Before approval, confirm source or lot release for the intended use, current vehicle and walking routes, receiver availability, tank headroom, and stock/version validity. Reserve the specific lot and litres against the plan version. Record loading and transfer custody. Send pickup staff their location, assignment volume, time window and verification contact. Give each school its order, expected arrival, volume and intended tank. Share a locally appropriate public notice with accessible directions, opening hours and a feedback contact. Publish only verified details through authorized channels. Use a cancellation/replacement notice when a closure or stock hold changes the plan.

      Retain the authorized plan version, reservations ledger, manifest or pickup instructions, recipient list, and acknowledgement deadline.

      6. Confirm delivery and recalculate service coverage

      Receivers confirm deliveries, the stock custodian reconciles volumes, and the dispatcher recalculates coverage.

      Record each tanker delivery at destination with order ID, lot, approved use, actually received litres, receiving tank, receiver reference, time, and variance reason. Increase usable destination stock only after receipt and any required acceptance. Track pickup allocations separately from actual handovers or collections. Keep unconfirmed volume in transit or under review until the custodian signs a reconciliation. Update site capacity and school runway from accepted stock and updated demand. Adjust the deadline and owner of each remaining fallback case.

      Save the receipts and applicable collection counts alongside the reconciliation status, updated gaps, and follow-up delivery decisions.

      7. Restore the repaired water point

      The technician submits evidence for the authorized water officer to review and sign.

      Log the completed repair and functional check with the asset and work order IDs. A pending status remains until the authorized officer records the locally applicable return-to-service decision, supporting references, permissible use, and validity. Keep closures or water holds in force where evidence is missing or expired. After release, notify caretakers, pickup staff, schools and other affected users through the original channels. Cancel only the backup orders already reviewed against new service availability and commitments.

      Keep the repair record, return-to-service reference, verification time, public notice, and documentation of any cancelled orders or released stock reservations. WHO emergency WASH guidance

      8. Hand over exceptions and learn from the incident

      The incident lead coordinates the handover with the incoming duty officer, school lead, and water authority.

      Review open household assistance, short school deliveries, water holds, complaints, missed collections and ledger discrepancies. For each unresolved row, record the next owner, action, due time, escalation route, and evidence required. Close the repair order independently when verified. Close the shared incident after all needs are resolved or accepted into named follow-up cases under local policy. Run a short debrief, compare outcomes with the agreed baseline, and correct maps and alert rules.

      Sign the shift handover, record who will handle remaining needs and feedback, and retain the final stock reconciliation.

      Create the ward map and verify it on the ground

      1. Start with a boundary and asset owners. Agree on the ward polygon, adjacent providers, source permissions, school catchments, and the authoritative registry. Import licensed road and boundary data. Log the source and date and preserve attribution. Assign stable local IDs even where the original map has no feature.
      2. Survey the service points. Capture GPS and accuracy, access and customary use, operator role, dependency area, service hours, and status. At pickup points record safe access, capacity per time window, opening hours, queuing space, and accessibility. At schools split public pickup storage, school drinking water, hygiene and sanitation reserves into separately identified stores. Record mixed school/shelter use and check for overlapping beneficiary counts.
      3. Build a routable graph. Split lines at real junctions. Record edge ID, from/to junctions, direction, travel mode, minutes, accessibility, restrictions, responsible verifier, last verified time, and expiry. Separate walking and tanker layers. A road can be open to foot travel and closed to a loaded vehicle. Check bridges, temporary crossings, ramps, steps, and turning or unloading space.
      4. Map need at group level. Give each settlement block or group a stable ID, approximate population, household count and timing of the count. Use approximate grouping inland from hazards, no household pins on public maps, and private referrals for individual assisted delivery where necessary. Record reported alternative access and assisted travel needs as group counts.
      5. Join assets and demand. Link water point to backup pickup options and affected groups, and school to its tanks and delivery route. Cross-check IDs, bounds, capacities, duplicate locations, and unreachable nodes. Simulate a closure and verify the detour with a community representative and the responsible field authority.
      6. Publish and refresh two views. Staff get restricted operational layers with verification timestamps. Public notices show approved pickup points, opening windows, locally verified directions, and a feedback contact. A route flagged closed, unknown or expired is excluded from automated public assignments until verified again. Confirm the update cadence at each incident briefing.

      For a Bangladesh pilot, identify the local Department of Public Health Engineering (DPHE), education, and disaster management counterparts and confirm the actual authority chain for that ward. The national emergency guidance provides a starting point. Bangladesh WASH Emergency Guidelines · OCHA responsible data sharing

      • New closure or security/access issue. Suspend affected assignments and manifests, verify alternate paths and update the graph. Reissue the revised plan and confirm affected groups and drivers heard the update.

      Shared records and software build order

      The existing three prototypes demonstrate the repair queue, routing model, and school volume allocation. The tabletop reuses those models on a common case and a fixed schematic. A field pilot needs a verified registry, approved operating procedures, and auditable records. Multi-device synchronization, production permissions and live dispatch are implementation work for the pilot.

      Recommended build order

      1. Digitize the registry. Create the asset, population-group, road link, site and tank layers. Use QGIS with a GeoPackage as a portable editing and map export format. Start a restricted incident register and stock ledger. Review identifiers, units and expected forms with duty staff.
      2. Make reporting offline-capable first. Configure Android ODK Collect and a partner-managed ODK Central instance where available. Give operators their own scoped access. Prepare simple, local-language forms for outages, road observations, school stock, pickup counts, and receipts. Preload known IDs and offline map layers. Test offline finalization, sent status, retries and reconciliation with numbered paper forms. Keep the duty board authoritative for operational decisions and reservations. ODK managing forms · ODK offline maps
      3. Connect the modules in shadow mode. Create import adapters that translate approved form submissions into common records and stable IDs. Run route eligibility and dependency checks. Write one consolidated proposal with its input snapshot, versions, constraints, model outputs and exceptions. Compare it with the dispatcher’s actual choices. Record why a recommendation was accepted or overridden.
      4. Add authenticated approvals and a transaction ledger. Use a partner-managed application service and PostgreSQL. Add PostGIS when spatial queries and mapped records need a common server. Expose role-limited actions for verification, plan approval, reservations, dispatch, receipts and return-to-service. Enforce those actions on the server. Secure evidence and contact details separately. PostGIS spatial queries
      5. Publish a separate read-only public view. Emit a dated notice from a verified plan revision. Include site, service window, access restrictions, update contact and an approved alternative where applicable. Use the locally appropriate channels and languages, including paper notices, calls, SMS or community announcements. Revoke superseded notices and log recipients and acknowledgement.
      6. Harden for multi-ward operations. Partition access by jurisdiction and keep ward policies configurable. Monitor stale map data, pending sync, expiring approval, unreconciled balances and notice delivery. Maintain protected backups, test restoration, and rehearse a rollback to paper and verbal dispatch. Hand the system and documentation to a named district owner and backup.

      State transitions and consistency rules

      • Repair order. Reported → verified → assigned → on site → repair complete pending clearance → authorized return to service → closed. Blocked, hold and reopen events preserve history and trigger review.
      • Dispatch order. Proposed → authorized and reserved → loaded / in transit → received or exception → reconciled. Cancellation releases only unused reservation. A new approved variant supersedes old orders and alerts the affected operators.
      • Detection and dispatch are separate. Any low-stock observation or route closure can open a review. Only authorized actors can change approved site status, release stock, reserve shared volume, publish a notice or close the repair.
      • Ledger balance. Track available, reserved, in transit, confirmed at destination and held or unreconciled. A movement shifts volume between buckets or posts a signed correction. Physical lot balance, each store’s capacity and active reservations are checked together. A published plan never decrements stock twice, and a repeat receipt cannot create water.
      • Server conflicts. Each event has a client-generated unique ID, an incident/target ID, actor, observed time, and expected version. The server deduplicates event IDs, stamps the accepted time, validates permissions and rejects stale writes for review. Put plan authorization and per-lot reservations in one database transaction with version checks/locking. Treat closure, hold and expired release as blocking states until an authorized review resolves them.
      • Data responsibility. Use minimum personal data, restricted contact and evidence storage, log retention agreed with the partner, and separate public datasets. Keep names and precise household coordinates out of exports used for demos or public maps. Keep role-scoped logs of approvals, overrides and exports. OCHA data sharing guidance

      Six-week ward pilot

      Start with an illustrative scope of 8–12 water points, two approved pickup locations plus one potential backup, 3–5 schools, and one transport provider. Use a trusted local implementing partner, an incident duty lead and backup, at least one technician, a route verifier, a school coordinator, two field staff, and a community inclusion liaison. People can hold multiple roles where local policy allows, with independent signoff for release and stock reconciliation.

      Acceptance drills before live use

      1. Duplicate report and reconnect. Send one offline event twice and two observations of the same outage. Expect one event application and a single verified repair order with both observations.
      2. Bridge closure and stale update. Close a link after a draft plan and bring an older open report. No approved assignment or public route uses the stale link. The old plan is held or superseded and the fallback is assigned.
      3. Scarce stock and concurrence. Two dispatchers try to reserve the same lot for different plans. At most one conflicting reservation succeeds. Remaining group-level and school gaps stay visible. No role bypass is accepted.
      4. Unreleased source, closed tanker route, or inaccessible point. Exercise each case separately. The system blocks the affected allocation or order and displays who can correct it. A manual dispatch still requires the local authority’s decision and a signed record.
      5. Partial delivery and delayed receipt. Dispatch a simulated load and receive less. Confirm only accepted litres increase destination stock. Unreconciled volume remains visible. Repeat the same receipt ID and check that stock remains unchanged.
      6. Mechanical repair with pending clearance. Record the repair but omit authorized release. The point remains unavailable for public service. On release, update notices while tracking any unreconciled distribution and open school needs.
      7. Accessibility and affordability. Take a route with a mobility user or representative, test the preferred public announcement channel in the local language, and check that fallback access and user charges follow the approved policy. Document alternatives for people unable to collect.

      Costs, handover, and the scale decision

      Build a line-item budget from staff time, translation and accessibility review, map work, devices/data, transport standby, pickup staffing, water safety checks, printing, hosting and maintenance. For practice budgeting only, 12 field person-days × BDT 1,500, 8 coordination days × BDT 2,000, 12 operator shifts × BDT 1,000, 4 vehicle trips × BDT 4,000, 6 verification checks × BDT 1,500, and BDT 5,000 for printing/data total BDT 76,000. A 15% buffer makes BDT 87,400. Replace all rates and quantities with partner-approved figures. Budget water procurement, major repairs, lab work, equipment and ongoing hosting separately where applicable.

      Before adding a ward, transfer the verified registry, local approval policy, user list, backup procedure, stock ledger, issue log, source code, and operating calendar to the designated owner. Train a second duty dispatcher and confirm the next ward’s source owners, demand assumptions and road verifier. Re-run the acceptance drills against that ward’s inventory and accessibility needs. Scale the common records and audit controls, with ward-specific data and policy.
